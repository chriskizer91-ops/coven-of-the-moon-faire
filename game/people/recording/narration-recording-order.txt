COVEN OF THE MOON FAIRE
VOICE RECORDING ORDER: THE NARRATOR, ZONES 1 TO 3
Made November 23, 2026. 48 lines, 1 speaker, about 4.6 minutes of speech.

WHAT THIS IS
These are new lines for the same small fantasy video game as the earlier recording orders (zones 1, 2 and 3). They are all said by one new speaker: the narrator. The narrator sets the scene at certain points: where the four witches are, what has just happened, what is in front of them. Each line below is recorded as its own sound file. The game finds a line's sound by its file name, so the names must be exact.

HOW TO MAKE THE FILES
 1. Use the same ElevenLabs model, the same settings and the same way of working as for the earlier recording orders of this game.
 2. One voice for the narrator, for all 48 lines in all three zones. Choose it from the voice note below, test it with the audition line (do not save the audition as a file), then keep that one voice and its settings throughout. It must not be a voice already used for a person in the game.
 3. Say exactly the words after "Say:". Do not add, drop or change a word.
 4. Words in [square brackets] are delivery cues, not words to speak. If a cue is spoken aloud by mistake, make the line again without the cue.
 5. "Tone:" says how the line should sound. It is never spoken. If a line comes out in the wrong tone, make it again; you may put a short cue from the tone note in square brackets at its start, such as [quietly] or [warmly].
 6. The narrator is calm throughout: a storyteller by a fire. Never dramatic, never a film-trailer voice, never hurried. If a line comes out theatrical, make it again plainer.
 7. One sound file for each line, named by its file name exactly, for example z1.narrator.03.mp3 (lower case, with the dots as shown).
 8. MP3, one channel (mono), 44.1 kHz, 64 kbps, as for the earlier orders. If a file comes out with two channels, mix it down to one.
 9. Silence: no more than a fifth of a second (0.2 s) at the start and at the end of each file. Trim any longer silence.
10. Loudness: the same for every file, and the same as the earlier orders: about -18 LUFS (integrated), with peaks no higher than -1 dBFS.
11. Packing: three zips, one for each zone, named z1-narrator.zip, z2-narrator.zip and z3-narrator.zip. Inside each: that zone's sound files, and a list named like z1-narrator-list.csv with two columns, file and seconds: each file's name and its real length.
12. Check before handing back: 48 files in 3 zips (18 in z1-narrator.zip, 15 in z2-narrator.zip, 15 in z3-narrator.zip), every file name below present once, and nothing else.

HOW TO SAY THE NAMES
Say them as in the earlier orders. If a name comes out wrong, write it in the International Phonetic Alphabet between slashes in the text.

SPEAKER: narrator   The narrator
ZIPS: z1-narrator.zip, z2-narrator.zip, z3-narrator.zip   (48 lines, about 278 seconds)
VOICE: A woman of about sixty. A fireside storyteller: warm, low and unhurried, close to the ear. Soft, plain British English with no strong region, so she belongs to no one town. Even and calm. She tells what is there and lets it be. Never dramatic, never sing-song, never hurried.
IF THAT DOES NOT SUIT: If a man reads better: about sixty, the same warmth and plainness, a low, dry, kind voice.
AUDITION LINE (to choose the voice, not a file): Night comes early to the fen. The people of Bogmire light their lamps, and wait.

ZONE 1: Wickhollow, the Thornwood and Bogmire   ZIP: z1-narrator.zip   (18 lines, about 104 seconds)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
z1.narrator.01.mp3
  Say:  Dusk, at the Moon Faire, in the fields outside Wickhollow. A moment ago, the captain’s twelve ship’s lamps tore loose and flew.
  Tone: The first words of the game. Warm and settled, drawing the listener in. A small pause after “Wickhollow”. No alarm at all in “tore loose and flew”: she is telling what happened.

z1.narrator.02.mp3
  Say:  The cottage on the hill, south of Wickhollow. Io grew up here. She has not stood at this gate in seven years.
  Tone: Quiet and plain. Three short facts, the last the slowest. No pity in it.

z1.narrator.03.mp3
  Say:  The edge of the Thornwood. The road to Bogmire goes in under the thorns. The last of the daylight does not.
  Tone: Even, a little dry on the last sentence. Not a warning, an observation.

z1.narrator.04.mp3
  Say:  Bogmire, standing on its stilts over the black water. Every lamp post in the town is empty.
  Tone: Low and still. “Empty” set down gently, like something put on a table.

z1.narrator.05.mp3
  Say:  The fen’s dark heart. The boardwalk ends here. No bird calls, and no frog.
  Tone: Hushed, close. Short sentences with room between them.

z1.narrator.06.mp3
  Say:  The great wraith comes apart like mist, and the lamps it wore lift free. Where it stood, one pale moth rises.
  Tone: Soft wonder, held back. Slower on “one pale moth rises”, lifting very slightly at the end.

z1.narrator.07.mp3
  Say:  The Grey Hat’s ship lifts from the lake, all twelve lamps lit and sewn fast. Below, Wickhollow grows small.
  Tone: Warm, with a little lift for the ship rising. The last sentence quieter, looking down.

z1.narrator.08.mp3
  Say:  The captain will not fly dark. His twelve lamps are out in the wilds tonight, and so are the wisps.
  Tone: Matter of fact, a trace of dryness. It tells the listener where to go without telling them to go.

z1.narrator.09.mp3
  Say:  A cold light drifts out of the trees, and turns toward the coven. A wisp.
  Tone: Quiet, watchful. “A wisp” very plain, almost under the breath.

z1.narrator.10.mp3
  Say:  The wisp is not harmed. It is let go, and where it was, a pale moth rises.
  Tone: Gentle and sure. The first sentence is the important one: say it kindly.

z1.narrator.11.mp3
  Say:  A ship’s lamp drifts down out of the dark, still lit, and settles in the grass. One of the twelve.
  Tone: A small, pleased surprise, kept quiet. “One of the twelve” counted, like the start of a tally.

z1.narrator.12.mp3
  Say:  Another of the captain’s lamps comes home.
  Tone: Light and brief. It will be heard several times: keep it easy, not weighty.

z1.narrator.13.mp3
  Say:  Eleven lamps. The twelfth is not in these woods.
  Tone: Even. A beat after “Eleven lamps”. The second sentence a shade lower.

z1.narrator.14.mp3
  Say:  The Moon Faire, by the well. The coven has been carried home, and wakes among friends.
  Tone: Kind and unworried. Losing is not a disaster here: say it like tucking someone in.

z1.narrator.15.mp3
  Say:  The coven rests by the Faire’s fires until their strength comes back.
  Tone: Warm, slow, easy. A long breath out.

z1.narrator.16.mp3
  Say:  Wickhollow. The market square is quiet tonight, and the town has lit its lamps early.
  Tone: Plain and fond, the way someone names a place they know well.

z1.narrator.17.mp3
  Say:  The jetty, on the lake. An airship rides at the end of the pier, with every one of her twelve lamp hooks bare.
  Tone: Even, with a little admiration for the ship. “Bare” set down plainly.

z1.narrator.18.mp3
  Say:  Something waits in the reeds ahead. The coven is not ready for it yet.
  Tone: Low and calm. A caution from someone on your side, not a threat.

ZONE 2: the Warm Roads and Dawnroost   ZIP: z2-narrator.zip   (15 lines, about 89 seconds)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
z2.narrator.01.mp3
  Say:  The ship flies north-west through the night, low over the hills. The cold is drinking her sunstone.
  Tone: Steady, a traveller’s voice. The second sentence a little wry.

z2.narrator.02.mp3
  Say:  The raiders fall away behind. Ahead, a few small fires mark the Warm Roads camp.
  Tone: Relief, kept small. The second sentence looks forward.

z2.narrator.03.mp3
  Say:  The Warm Roads camp. The Ember Wardens laid these roads to stay warm underfoot the whole year round.
  Tone: Plain telling, with a little pride in the Wardens’ work. It does not say the roads are cold: the coven finds that.

z2.narrator.04.mp3
  Say:  The first of the three dark nodes: a road stone, by the bridge. It should be warm to the hand.
  Tone: Quiet and exact. The last sentence slower, a small sadness under it, not pressed.

z2.narrator.05.mp3
  Say:  Io’s small flame catches. The stone warms, and the frost draws back from the road around it.
  Tone: Gentle and glad, like watching a fire take. Unhurried.

z2.narrator.06.mp3
  Say:  Dawnroost, the Ember Wardens’ town. Eight years ago, forty Wardens marched north from here. None has come back.
  Tone: Even and grave without heaviness. Three plain facts. Do not lean on “none”.

z2.narrator.07.mp3
  Say:  The Wardens’ hall. Two long rows of bunks, under the rafters.
  Tone: Hushed, as on stepping into a quiet room.

z2.narrator.08.mp3
  Say:  The hill behind the hall, at night. Dawnroost’s last living node burns low here, the only warm thing for miles.
  Tone: Low and close. Warmth on “the only warm thing”.

z2.narrator.09.mp3
  Say:  The node holds. Down in the town, one after another, the road stones begin to glow.
  Tone: Quiet gladness. “One after another” unhurried, as if watching them come on.

z2.narrator.10.mp3
  Say:  The ship lifts into the cold, and turns north. Behind her, every window of Dawnroost’s hall is lit.
  Tone: Steady, moving on. The last sentence warmer, a look back.

z2.narrator.11.mp3
  Say:  Three nodes of the Ember Line have gone dark along the road: by the bridge, among the standing stones, and up on the rise.
  Tone: Clear and useful, like directions given by a friend. Each of the three places distinct.

z2.narrator.12.mp3
  Say:  Two nodes lit. One is left.
  Tone: Brief and even. A tally.

z2.narrator.13.mp3
  Say:  All three nodes are lit. The road to Dawnroost is warm underfoot again.
  Tone: Pleased, quietly. “Warm underfoot again” said with comfort.

z2.narrator.14.mp3
  Say:  The Warm Roads camp, by the roadwarden’s fire. The coven wakes, sore and safe.
  Tone: Kind and unworried. A small smile on “sore and safe”.

z2.narrator.15.mp3
  Say:  Whatever comes for the node at night, the coven is not ready to meet it yet.
  Tone: Low and calm. A caution from someone on your side.

ZONE 3: the northern wilds and the shipyard   ZIP: z3-narrator.zip   (15 lines, about 84 seconds)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
z3.narrator.01.mp3
  Say:  North again, into colder air. Frost furs the rigging, and the twelve lamps burn small.
  Tone: Steady, a little hushed by the cold. Unhurried.

z3.narrator.02.mp3
  Say:  The raiders drop behind. Below, the wilds begin.
  Tone: Brief relief, then looking down and ahead.

z3.narrator.03.mp3
  Say:  The northern camp. A ring of tents on frozen ground, and past the last tent, the wilds.
  Tone: Plain and spare, like the place.

z3.narrator.04.mp3
  Say:  The shipyard of the moon-folk. Tall hulls stand on the slips, and there is a queue at every gangway.
  Tone: A little awe at the ships, then dry on the queue.

z3.narrator.05.mp3
  Say:  The north road, where the brambles have closed over it.
  Tone: Even and quiet. Nothing sinister: it is simply so.

z3.narrator.06.mp3
  Say:  The edge of Eldergrove. Here the frost stops, as if at a wall, and the leaves are green.
  Tone: Soft surprise, and ease. Warmer than anything since Wickhollow.

z3.narrator.07.mp3
  Say:  Night, under Eldergrove’s eaves. The coven makes a small fire, and keeps it small.
  Tone: Close and restful. A trace of meaning on “keeps it small”, not pressed.

z3.narrator.08.mp3
  Say:  The northern crossroads. Four roads meet here, under snow.
  Tone: Still and spare. Leave a silence after it.

z3.narrator.09.mp3
  Say:  The knight lowers her sword. She turns, and walks back up the north road, the way she came.
  Tone: Quiet, watching her go. No triumph. Slow on “the way she came”.

z3.narrator.10.mp3
  Say:  The shipmaster’s tall house, under its roof of glass. She has just said a name.
  Tone: Hushed. The second sentence very simple, and then stop: the silence after it matters.

z3.narrator.11.mp3
  Say:  The ship climbs away from the shipyard, new-rigged, and turns north-east, toward the mountains.
  Tone: Steady and resolved. The mountains are where the story is going: say it plainly.

z3.narrator.12.mp3
  Say:  The ship is up on the slip, and will be for a while. North of the yard, a road runs toward Eldergrove.
  Tone: Easy, a little dry about the wait. The second sentence helpful.

z3.narrator.13.mp3
  Say:  Past the grove, the north road goes on to a crossroads.
  Tone: Plain directions, kindly given.

z3.narrator.14.mp3
  Say:  The northern camp. The coven wakes in a tent on the frozen ground, sore and safe.
  Tone: Kind and unworried. A small smile on “sore and safe”.

z3.narrator.15.mp3
  Say:  Something bright is walking the north road. The coven is not ready to stand in its way yet.
  Tone: Low and calm. A caution from someone on your side.
