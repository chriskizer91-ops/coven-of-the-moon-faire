## 1. What was looked for first, and what was found

A name Chris already gave beats a new one. So all four read-only sources were searched for any name of a star, a sun,
a planet, a moon, a season, a calendar, a god or a sky-story.

**No star and no other planet has a name anywhere.** The old Envoi's star map notes say so twice ("Names wanted: the
three stars and the four other planets"), and Moon Faire's notes say the planets' names "are still open".

What does exist, and was kept clear of:

another of his repos, and that it "may have" these names. That repo is not a source for this game, so it was not
opened. **If Chris knows he named a star or a planet there, his name wins.**

## 2. How the names are made

Two rules, so the sky reads as one people's sky.

1. **A light gets a lamp's name.** This is a lamp-and-candle world, and its sky already has plain names: the Aether,
   the Thinning. So the three stars are named the way a lamplighter would name them, each with "the" in front.
2. **A world gets a world's name, and the fair folk give it a second one.** Thareia and Auros are old, soft, single
   words. The four other planets get names of that kind for the charts. Then, because this is the Moon Faire's game,
   each also has the name the fair folk call it by: the four wanderers are **a fair on the road**, and each is one of
   the company.

No two names start with the same sound. None is the name of a real star, planet or moon. Section 5 says honestly what
each is near to.

What each one looks like is taken from the star map as it stands. Those looks were made up for the map on November 17
and are Chris's to change. If a look changes, its name should be looked at again.

## 3. The names

### The two suns: the Cresset and the Taper

- **The big gold sun: the Cresset** (KRESS-it). A cresset is an iron basket of fire on a tall pole, the kind that
  lights a town square. It is the great common fire everyone works by. Sunstone is its colour.
  - Other choices: **the Hearth** (see the note below); **the Daylamp**.
- **The small blue-white sun: the Taper** (TAY-per). A taper is the thin candle a lamplighter carries to light the
  others. It is small, sharp and bright, and it never stops going round the big one, as if it were still lighting it.
  - Other choices: **the Flint**; **the Spark**.
- **What people call the pair:** on the map, **the Cresset and the Taper**. In plain talk they are still **the twin
  suns**, and daylight is still just "the sun", so the line already recorded stays true.
  - Other choices for one name for both: **the Daystars**; or leave the map's label as **the Twin Suns** and keep the
    two names inside the card.
- Why it follows: the people who light lamps for a living would see their own trade in the sky. Lamplighters, wardens
  and airship captains all live by kept flames.
- Card: *Two suns that circle each other at the heart of the system: the Cresset, big and gold, and the Taper, small
  and blue-white. Every world orbits the pair, and ships' crystals charge from their light.*
  Small line under it: *A cresset is a basket of fire on a pole. A taper is the thin candle that lights it.*

A note on **the Hearth**. It is the warmest word, and Chris's Aethermoor already loves it (Hearthstone Keep, the
Hearthsea). That is also why it is second: a sun called the Hearth would make it sound as if Hearthstone Keep were
named after the sun, which reaches into his other games. If he likes that, it is a fine first choice.

### The far third star: the Vigil

- **The Vigil** (VIJ-il). A vigil is a watch kept through the night, and the small light kept burning for it.
- Other choices: **the Waylight**; **the Old Ember** (lovely, but it would sit too close to the Ember Line and the
  Ember Wardens, and could be taken to mean something about them).
- Why it follows: it is a great distance out and still part of the system. On the map it burns red, like a coal kept
  in overnight. It is the light that stays at the edge and keeps watch.
- Card: *The third star, red as a coal. It sits a great distance out but is still part of the system, keeping its
  watch from the edge. Its light charges the crystals too.*
  Small line under it: *A vigil is a watch kept through the night.*

### The four other planets

In order outward from the suns. Thareia lies between the second and the third.

**The map's "Unnamed I": Brasa, which the fair folk call the Fire-Eater**

- **Brasa** (BRAH-sah). An old word for a live coal.
- Other choices: **Scoria** (SKOR-ee-ah); **Cindrel** (SIN-drel). For the fair name: **the Stoker**; **the
  Coal-Carrier**.
- Why: it is the smallest world and the nearest to the suns, dark and cracked, with fire showing through. From the
  ground it would be a red spark that never strays far from the suns. The fair's fire-eater stands closest to the fire.
- Card: *The world nearest the suns: small, dark and cracked, with fire showing through like a live coal. From Thareia
  it is a red spark low in the dawn or the dusk.*
  Small line: *The fair folk call it the Fire-Eater.*

**The map's "Unnamed II": Saffren, which the fair folk call the Outrider**

- **Saffren** (SAFF-ren). From saffron, the gold dye and spice that comes a long way to a fair.
- Other choices: **Marram** (MA-rum, the grass that holds dunes together); **Zarrin** (ZAR-in). For the fair name:
  **the Herald**; **the Lamplighter** (apt, since it shows when lamps are lit, but Old Bridie already has that trade).
- Why: a world of gold dunes and canyons with a few small seas. It lies inside Thareia's path, so from the ground it
  would be the bright light seen just before the suns come up or just after they go down. A fair sends an outrider
  ahead to find the next ground.
- Card: *A world of gold dunes and deep canyons, with a few small blue-green seas. It is the bright light that rides
  ahead of the suns at dawn and follows them down at dusk.*
  Small line: *The fair folk call it the Outrider.*

**The map's "Unnamed IV": Pavane, which the fair folk call the Hoop-Dancer**

- **Pavane** (pah-VAHN). A pavane is a slow, grave dance, walked in procession.
- Other choices: **Wistral** (WISS-tral, from wisteria, for its colour); **Gossamer**. For the fair name: **the
  Ringmaster**; **the Juggler** (but it sounds too like the Jester).
- Why: it is the giant, twice Thareia's width on the map, banded in lilac, cream and rose, with a wide ring round it,
  and it goes round slowly. The fair folk see a dancer in a hoop. The charts name the way it moves.
- Card: *The giant of the system, banded in lilac, cream and rose, with a wide ring round it. It crosses the sky
  slowly and gravely, like the old dance it is named for.*
  Small line: *The fair folk call it the Hoop-Dancer.*

**The map's "Unnamed V": Glaswen, which the fair folk call the Straggler**

- **Glaswen** (GLASS-wen). It sounds like what it is: a world of blue-white glass.
- Other choices: **Rimewyn** (RIME-win); **Isenne** (ee-SEN). For the fair name: **the Last Wagon**; **the Latecomer**.
- Why: the farthest known world, pale and frozen, with a cold blue glow in the cracks of its ice. It is the slowest
  of them all, so the fair folk see the one at the back of the road.
- Card: *The farthest known world, pale and frozen, with a cold blue glow in the cracks of its ice. It is the slowest
  on the road: more than three of Thareia's years to go round once.*
  Small line: *The fair folk call it the Straggler.*

### The band of free crystal: the Amberwake

- **The Amberwake** (AM-ber-wake). A wake is the bright trail something leaves behind it.
- Other choices: **the Amber Road**; or keep **the Crystal Drift** as it is.
- Why: it is a band of amber stone circling the suns, out past the giant. Crystal holds the light of the stars, so a
  lamplighter's story for it writes itself: the Taper carried fire out to light the Vigil, and this is what it spilled
  on the way. The word "Drift" is not lost: it is what captains call it.
- Card: *Aether crystal isn't only found inside planets. Here the amber stone floats free, a band of it circling the
  suns. Lamplighters say the Taper spilled it, carrying fire out to light the Vigil.*
  Small line: *Captains call it the Drift.*
  (The first two sentences are the card's words as they stand now. Only the third is new.)

## 4. What this adds to the lore

Every new fact the names bring with them. Chris can say yes or no to each. None of it is about the story's people,
and none of it changes anything settled.

1. **The two suns are told apart by colour and size:** a big gold one and a small blue-white one. (This is the map's
   look, not Chris's word.)
2. **People on Thareia use cressets and tapers,** so the names mean something to them.
3. **In plain talk people still say "the sun"** for the daylight of both together.
4. **The far star is red,** and people think of it as keeping watch. (Red is the map's look.)
5. **The lamplighters have one small story:** the Taper carried fire out to light the Vigil, and what it spilled is the
   band of free crystal. It is told as a story ("lamplighters say"), not as what happened.
6. **Captains call the band "the Drift".**
7. **The fair folk have their own names for the four planets,** as a fair on the road: the Fire-Eater, the Outrider,
   the Hoop-Dancer, the Straggler.
8. **What each planet is like** (all four are the map's looks):
   - Brasa: the nearest to the suns, small, dark and cracked, with fire in the cracks.
   - Saffren: gold dunes, deep canyons, a few small blue-green seas.
   - Pavane: the giant, banded in lilac, cream and rose, with a ring.
   - Glaswen: the farthest known, frozen, with a blue glow in its ice.
9. **What two of them look like from the ground:** Brasa is a red spark low at dawn or dusk; Saffren is the bright
   light before sunrise and after sunset. (Both follow from where the map puts them, inside Thareia's path.)
10. **A slow dance called the pavane is known on Thareia.**
11. **Glaswen takes more than three of Thareia's years to go round the suns.** (By the map's own rule it is 3.4.)

Not added, on purpose: who named the worlds; whether anyone lives on them; anything about gods; anything about
Halcyon, the Ember Wardens, the coven or the Grey Hat.

### Three ideas left out, in case Chris wants one

They follow from the names, but each is a bigger step, so none is in a card.

- **A month from the suns.** Auros takes 500 years to go round, so it cannot give Thareia its months. One turn of the
  Taper round the Cresset could: by the map's own rule that is about two weeks.
- **The Vigil comes round once in a lifetime.** By the map's own rule it would take about 55 years to creep once round
  the sky.
- **The Ember Line and the Ember Wardens** could take their name from the red star. That touches the story's people,
  so it is only a question.

## 5. Is each name clear of real and well-known names?

Checked against what the writer knows, with a web search for each first choice. "Clear" means: not a real star,
planet or moon, and not a well-known one from someone else's story or game.

| Name | Clear? | What it is near to |
|---|---|---|
| The Cresset | Clear | A real old word. **Said aloud it is close to "crescent"**, which matters in a game about a moon |
| The Taper | Clear | A real word for a thin candle |
| The Vigil | Clear | A real word. A search found a small world called Vigil in the Traveller tabletop game |
| Brasa | Clear | The Spanish and Portuguese word for a live coal |
| Saffren | Clear | Saffron. Saffron City is a town in Pokémon |
| Pavane | **Not sure** | A real word for a dance. A search found a 1968 novel, *A Planet Called Pavanne*, and a world called Pavanne in Traveller. Neither is well known, and both spell it with two n's |
| Glaswen | Clear | Nothing found |
| The Amberwake | Clear | Nothing found. It starts with the letter A, like Auros and the Aether, but not with their sound |
| The Fire-Eater, the Hoop-Dancer | Clear | Plain fair words |
| The Outrider | Clear | *Outriders* is a 2021 video game (the name of the game, not of a world) |
| The Straggler | **Not sure** | "Blue straggler" is what astronomers call one real kind of star. Not a star's name, but close to the sky |
| Wistral (a second choice) | Not sure | A search found a small Star Wars planet called Wistril |

The search only showed the titles of the pages it found (a book list for the novel, the Traveller game's own wiki
for Pavanne and Vigil, a Star Wars wiki for Wistril). The pages themselves were not read. Their addresses are in the
job's report to the hub, not here, since no page in this repo carries a web address.

Sounds, so none is mixed up when heard: KRESS, TAY, VIJ, BRAH, SAFF, pah-VAHN, GLASS, AM. Beside them: Thareia,
Auros (OR-oss on the story page).
