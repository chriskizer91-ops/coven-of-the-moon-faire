## The opening

In the camp, the moment the game begins. The twelve lamps have just flown.

- **Callisto** (the opening; 3.4 s; `z1.callisto.01`): “Every flame in the camp is leaning north-east.” *Quiet and exact. The first to see it. Not alarmed, just sure.*
- **Kore** (the opening; 5.0 s; `z1.kore.01`): “Twelve lamps leaving all at once. Someone’s putting on a better show than ours.” *Delighted, a performer admiring a rival act. The laugh comes first and is real. Under it, a flicker she covers.* Said as: `[laughs] Twelve lamps leaving all at once. Someone’s putting on a better show than ours.`

## Odile, the lamp-seller

In the camp. The first guide. Her small story: a beginning, a turn and an end.

- **Odile** (first talk; 3.3 s; `z1.odile.01`): “Don’t look at me. Look at my flames.” *Calm, dry, a little stern. She means it kindly, and she is not joking.*
- **Odile** (dusk or asked, a later talk; 6.2 s; `z1.odile.02`): “The cold lights? Flames that leaned too far, I’d guess, and never stood back up.” *Thoughtful, half to herself. A trade secret, offered as a guess.*
- **Odile** (home or fen; 5.9 s; `z1.odile.03`): “I’ve put the shutters up. I won’t sell a lamp I can’t promise will stay.” *Plain and proud, a little ashamed of the shutters. The last word firm.*
- **Odile** (after; 5.2 s; `z1.odile.04`): “They lean a finger less than at dusk. The shutters can come down.” *Dry, quietly pleased. She will not make more of it than that.*

## Tully, the gate keeper

One ordinary person’s full six: a hello; early, middle and late; the line that points the way; and the call as the coven passes the gate.

- **Tully** (first talk; 6.1 s; `z1.tully.01`): “I count everything through this gate. Tonight, something went out that never came in.” *Flat, careful, slow. Unsettled, and hiding it. The last three words matter most.*
- **Tully** (dusk or asked, a later talk; 6.8 s; `z1.tully.02`): “Every cold light leaves by the same road. At my gate, that means the show’s over.” *Dry and certain, someone who has watched a thousand crowds go home. Held as a guess, not news.*
- **Tully** (dusk, a later talk; 6.6 s; `z1.tully.03`): “The lamps were the captain’s. He’s at the jetty, past the town, on the lake.” *Brisk directions, a little put out about the lamps. Clear on the place.*
- **Tully** (home or fen; 6.1 s; `z1.tully.04`): “I’ve kept the gate open past closing. For you four. Don’t tell the watchman.” *Gruff and a little embarrassed. The warmth is in the small stop before “for you four”.*
- **Tully** (after; 5.0 s; `z1.tully.05`): “Out and back, every time. I’ve stopped counting you four. Nearly.” *The thaw. Dry, almost fond. A small stop before “nearly”, which is a joke.*
- **Tully** (any time; 2.3 s; `z1.tully.06`): “Four out. Bring back four.” *Called after them as they pass the gate. Short and plain. Not quite a blessing.*

## The Grey Hat

At the jetty. His first talk asks for the lamps. His last has a line of Europa’s in it.

- **The Grey Hat** (first talk; 4.8 s; `z1.captain.01`): “You’ll be the Faire’s four. Good. I’ve lost my whole crew.” *Flat and clipped, deadpan. Let “crew” land and hang there.*
- **The Grey Hat** (first talk; 5.7 s; `z1.captain.02`): “Twelve lamps, torn from their hooks. Bring them home. I don’t lift dark.” *Short and firm, no pleading. The last three words are a rule he has kept all his life.*
- **The Grey Hat** (asked to fen, some lamps home, a later talk; 4.6 s; `z1.captain.03`): “I flew dark once. Young, and sure of myself. Once.” *Very quiet, no self-pity. A long breath before the last word, which is the whole story.* Said as: `[quietly] I flew dark once. Young, and sure of myself. Once.`
- **Europa** (after, all twelve lamps home; 5.3 s; `z1.europa.01`): “All twelve, sewn down in blue. Lean all you like, little ones. You’re staying.” *Light and quick, teasing the flames as if they were kittens. Pleased with her work.* Said as: `[playfully] All twelve, sewn down in blue. Lean all you like, little ones. You’re staying.`
- **The Grey Hat** (after, all twelve lamps home; 4.6 s; `z1.captain.04`): “Any sailor with sense would fly the other way. Get aboard.” *Gruff and decided. A smile he will not let anyone see.*

## Nettie, the herb-seller

In Wickhollow. She has kept an eye on the cottage, and she knew both sisters.

- **Nettie** (first talk; 4.3 s; `z1.nettie.01`): “Io. Seven summers I’ve watered your garden, and not one letter.” *A fond scold that does not hold. Glad to see her, and cross about being glad.*
- **Nettie** (asked to fen, a later talk; 6.8 s; `z1.nettie.02`): “The morning your sister went north, Io, she bought heartsease off me. She said it was for you.” *Gentle and careful, telling something she has kept a long time. Slower at the end.* Said as: `The morning your sister went north, Io, she bought heartsease off me. [gently] She said it was for you.` Rests on call 2.
- **Nettie** (home or fen; 5.8 s; `z1.nettie.03`): “If anyone’s still in Bogmire, it’s Old Wenna. Past the Thornwood, out in the fen.” *Practical, a little worried. Directions given like a recipe.*
- **Nettie** (after; 5.8 s; `z1.nettie.04`): “Go on with the Faire, Io. I’ll keep the garden. Gardens wait better than people.” *Warm, letting her go. A small smile on the last line, so it does not break.*

## At the cottage

The first time the coven comes to Io’s cottage.

- **Io** (at the cottage; 2.7 s; `z1.io.01`): “Her boots are still by the door.” *Almost a whisper. She did not expect it to hurt. Keep it small.* Said as: `[softly] Her boots are still by the door.` Rests on call 2.

## After the great wraith

In the fen, as the great wraith is released and a pale moth rises.

- **Io** (after the great wraith; 3.5 s; `z1.io.02`): “Look, Kore. Every lamp in Bogmire, going home.” *Relief, counting them in. A little out of breath, the fight just over.*
- **Kore** (after the great wraith; 3.2 s; `z1.kore.02`): “Never mind the lamps, Io. Watch the moth.” *Soft and rapt, the only still moment she has. Not a rebuke. An invitation.* Said as: `[softly] Never mind the lamps, Io. Watch the moth.`

## How each might sound (a first guess for casting)

- **Odile, the lamp-seller.** A woman of about sixty-five. A light French accent worn soft by years on the road. Low-middle pitch, even and unhurried, a little husky. Dry, precise, fond. To audition with: “Hold it still. There. See how it sits? That’s a good wick.”
- **Tully, the gate keeper.** A woman in her fifties. Flat, careful Northern English. Low pitch, slow, sparing. Every word counted out like a coin. Warmth arrives late and small. To audition with: “Name. Coin. In you go. Next.”
- **The Grey Hat.** A man of about forty-five. Low and a little weathered, a faint east-coast Scots burr. Slow, clipped, deadpan. Says less than he means. To audition with: “Rope’s wet. Sky’s clear. Nobody touches the wheel but me.”
- **Nettie, the herb-seller.** A woman of about sixty. Soft West Country English (all of Wickhollow sounds like this). Middle pitch, quick and warm. A scold that melts. To audition with: “Mint for your belly, sage for your throat, and a talking-to for free.”
- **Io, the Herbal Witch.** A woman of about thirty. Warm, low-middle, gentle and unhurried, with a faint trace of Wickhollow under years of travel. Soft, with steel under it. To audition with: “Hold still. This will sting, and then it won’t.”
- **Kore, the Jester.** A woman of about thirty. Bright and quick, a trained stage voice that can drop to a hush. Laughter always close under the words. To audition with: “Everyone laughs at the fool. That’s how she gets close enough to tell the truth.”
- **Europa, the Fox.** A woman in her late twenties. Light, quick and playful, middle-high, a smile in it. A slight Irish lilt. To audition with: “Snip, there. Oh, don’t sulk. It’s a better shape now.”
- **Callisto, the Ranger.** A woman in her mid twenties. Low, calm and measured. Few words, each one meant. A faint accent like nobody else in the game. To audition with: “Wait. There. Now.”
