# The story brief: what the story job writes from

**Status: a proposal by the hub (Claude), November 19, 2026. Not approved.** Chris asked the hub to put the lore
together for a fresh conversation that writes the story's dialogue. Lines marked **Settled** are Chris's own word.
Everything else is the hub's proposal and stays one until Chris says yes. Where this brief and Chris disagree, Chris
wins.

## 1. Settled (Chris's word)

**The world**
- The world is Thareia. Aethermoor is one of its continents, and the game happens on it. Its moon is **Auros**, which
  goes round Thareia once every 500 years, hanging almost still in the sky.
- **The Moon Faire** is a normal travelling fair. It is called that because it follows the moon round the world. It
  is the most mixed company there is: every trade, country and way of living. It has come to Aethermoor.
- A Dark Ages fantasy: lamps and candles, airships that fly on amber sunstone (the same stone as aether crystal).
  Space is the aether, not a vacuum. The moon's people (the Aurosi) are the wealthy ones.
- **A soul is a light. A wisp is a light that has lost its way.**
- This game is an alternate universe to the first *Envoi on the Longest Night*. That game's retired lore stays out:
  the Drowned Mother, Old Snuff, the mandrakes, Mother's Hollow.

**The coven** (four separate witches who work together; all four in the party from the start, with a shared past;
the player can walk as any of them)
- **Kore, the Jester.** The strongest. The duality of life and death, and infinity: "literally Doom". She laughs,
  she steps in front and takes the blows, and her spell (Sealed Fate, then Curtain Call) ends fights.
- **Europa, the Fox.** Chaos, and the fabric of reality. A playful fabric artist: scissors, needle, thread and a bell.
  Costume fox ears. Cold blue foxfire. She can make a foe strike itself, and she is how the party gets away.
- **Io, the Herbal Witch.** Healing. The red horned hat, herbs, strong heals, and summons with a paladin's energy.
- **Callisto, the Ranger.** The moment. A princess with a bow, a sword and knives. The weakest magic, the best blade.
- Strongest magic first: Kore, Europa, Io, Callisto. Moon phases are no part of their magic.
- Io and Kore are very close friends, not sisters.

**The game**
- It starts at the Moon Faire's camp, outside Wickhollow. The fair folk are themselves, at the camp. The townsfolk
  are the new Envoi's seventeen. A few important people may be played by the doll. Every line is written new.
- The Grey Hat is the airship captain, a friend of the Faire. He captains the ship that flies between zones. (His
  name may be "Noiz": not settled.)
- Four zones, with a gate fight at levels 5, 10, 15 and 20: the great wraith at Bogmire; Dawnroost; Halcyon; and
  **the fire that would not end, at Misthollow's dead Moonwell**, in two forms. What the fire *is* was left for
  the story.
- Io's cottage, south of Wickhollow, "is like her family's cottage ... maybe" (Chris, November 18).
- **Every person speaks aloud** (ElevenLabs voices). Everyone has at least five or six lines. A few people have many
  more, and the story should lead the player to them. The size of the sound has to be kept down. (Chris, November 19.)

**From the draft written with Chris for the new Envoi** (November 16; a draft, but his hand is in it)
- Io's magic is asked for, never owned: plants answer because she tends them, old powers because she keeps her vows.
  She takes nothing that is not freely given. She has saved many people and not all, and she writes the ones she
  lost letters she has nowhere to send. **She holds on.**
- Kore has been below and walked back out laughing. She sees how every story ends. She is a licensed fool, the one
  who may say true things. She has never had anything she could not bear to lose, until Io. **She lets go.**
- A beaten wisp or wraith is not killed. It is released, and a pale moth rises. A Shadow Wraith is what a wisp
  becomes when nobody helps it.
- Io's prayers: Lunara the moth goddess; the Elderwood Guardian, a stag of living wood; and Envoi, the Letter Wyrm,
  folded from her unsent letters, its heart lit by Kore's bells.

## 2. The hub's proposal: the story in ten lines

1. Auros is low in Aethermoor's west. These are the land's last moonlit years: after them, about 250 years of dark.
2. The Moon Faire follows the moon, so it reaches each land last. Its coming is a festival and a goodbye. It has
   pitched its tents outside Wickhollow.
3. For Io this is home. She grew up in the cottage on the hill south of Wickhollow, left with the Faire years ago,
   and has not been back.
4. Eight years ago her older sister **Halcyon**, a paladin of the Ember Wardens, went north so the dark would never
   come. In Misthollow's Moonwell she lit a fire that would not end. Io believes she is dead. The oldest of Io's
   unsent letters is to her.
5. The fire calls every light: lamp flames lean toward it with no wind, the warmth leaves the Warm Roads, and the
   souls of the dead answer it instead of the moon. A light that answers goes cold on the way. Those are the wisps.
6. On the night the game opens, every flame at the Faire leans north-east. Wisps drift through the camp. The Grey
   Hat's twelve ship's lamps tear off their hooks and fly. He will not fly dark.
7. The coven goes after the lamps: through Wickhollow and past Io's cottage, through the Thornwood, to Bogmire,
   already dark, where the great wraith sits in the fen with the town's lamps in its ribs.
8. Released, it lets the lamps go home. But every flame still leans north-east. The ship carries the coven on: the
   cold Warm Roads and Dawnroost; the northern wilds, where a knight of light fights with Io's own spells; the
   frozen pass and Misthollow.
9. At the dead Moonwell each of the four gives what is hers. Callisto holds the one moment. Europa, who mends
   everything, cuts the threads that hold each light. Kore draws the fire's hunger onto herself and gives it its
   Curtain Call. Io sends her letters at last, and each held soul follows its own name home.
10. One small flame is left, and it is kept. The moon sets, the stars come out, and the Faire moves on.

**The one idea: the dark is not the enemy. A light that will not let go is.** The dark is a season, like winter.
The danger is someone who loves too much to let the night come. That is Halcyon's fire, and it is Io's own flaw
grown to the size of a mountain.

## 3. Each witch's thread (proposal)

- **Io holds on.** Home, the cottage, the sister, the letters. What she learns: to let go.
- **Kore lets go.** She sees every ending, but the fire stops endings, so she cannot see anything it holds. For the
  first time she does not know how a story comes out, and she finds she cares. What she learns: to defend.
- **Europa mends.** She sees the call as threads pulling every flame, and she can sew a flame to its wick with blue
  thread. What she learns: some cloth has to be cut.
- **Callisto waits for the moment.** She is the first to see the flames lean. She is a princess from a land the
  moon left long ago, so she is the only one of the four who knows the dark can be lived in. "It is a season."

## 5. How talk will work in the game (the hub builds this)

- The player walks up and taps Talk. One talk is one or two lines, at most about ten seconds, spoken aloud with the
  words on the screen. A story talk with one of the four people in bold can be a short exchange of up to six lines,
  with one or two of them a witch's.
- There are no choices to pick. A line is said once before any repeats.
- The same recording plays whichever witch the player walks as. So no line may say "you" and mean one witch. All
  four are always there: "you four", "the coven", or a witch by name.
- A person never states as fact what a wisp is. Each has a guess. Io and Kore know.

## 6. Voices and size

- A line is one breath: about 12 words, never more than 24. About five seconds.
- Zone 1 is about 2,600 words, about 17 minutes of speech. As MP3 at 64 kbps that is about 8 MB, about 11 MB inside
  the one-file build. The whole game should stay near an hour of speech.
- Each speaker gets a voice note for casting: age, sex, where they sound like they are from, pitch, pace, texture,
  and one line to audition with. Places share a sound: the Faire is travellers from everywhere; Wickhollow sounds
  alike; Bogmire sounds alike, and different from Wickhollow.
- ElevenLabs' newer models (Eleven v3 and v4) take delivery cues in square brackets, such as `[whispers]`,
  `[sighs]`, `[laughs]`. Keep them in their own field, apart from the words shown on the screen, and use few.
- **A name costs a new recording to change.** No line says a name Chris has not approved. Other people speak of the
  fair folk by trade ("the lamp-seller"). The Grey Hat is "the captain" or "the Grey Hat".
