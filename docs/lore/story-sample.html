The story in ten lines

  1. Auros is low in Aethermoor's west. These are the land's last moonlit years. After them come about 250 years of dark, as they come to every land in turn.
  2. The Moon Faire follows the moon from behind, at the edge of its light, so it reaches each land last. Its coming is a festival and a goodbye. It has pitched its tents outside Wickhollow.
  3. For Io this is home. She grew up in the cottage on the hill south of the town. Seven years ago she went off with the Faire, and she has not been back.
  4. Eight years ago her older sister Halcyon, a paladin of the Ember Wardens, went north so the dark would never come. In Misthollow's Moonwell she lit a fire that would not end. Nobody heard from her again. Io believes she is dead. The oldest of Io's unsent letters is to her.
  5. The fire calls every light. Lamp flames lean toward it with no wind, and the warmth drains out of the Warm Roads. The lights of the dead should rise to the moon, but the moon is sinking and the fire is nearer. A light that answers the fire goes cold on the way. Those are the wisps.
  6. On the night the game opens, every flame at the Faire leans north-east, and Callisto is the first to see it. Wisps drift through the camp. The Grey Hat's twelve ship's lamps tear off their hooks and fly. He will not fly dark.
  7. The coven goes after the lamps: through Wickhollow, where Nettie the herb-seller has kept Io's garden; past the cottage, still as her sister left it; through the Thornwood; to Bogmire, already dark, where the great wraith sits in the fen with the town's lamps in its ribs.
  8. Released, it lets the lamps go home, and a pale moth rises toward the moon. But every flame still leans. Europa sews the twelve flames to their wicks with blue thread, and the ship carries the coven on: the cold Warm Roads and Dawnroost; the northern wilds, where a knight of light fights with Io's own spells; the frozen pass and Misthollow.
  9. At the dead Moonwell each of the four gives what is hers. Callisto holds the one moment. Europa, who sewed the flames down, cuts the threads that hold each light. Kore draws the fire's hunger onto herself and gives it its Curtain Call. Io sends her letters at last, and each held soul follows its own name home. The oldest letter she does not send. The knight is her sister, alive, and Io gives it to her by hand.
  10. One small flame is left, and it is kept. The moon sets, the stars come out, and the Faire moves on.

The one idea (the hub's, kept): the dark is not the enemy. A light that will not let go is.

How each of these eight might sound

A first guess for casting, so you can hear the lines in your head. Every voice is yours to change; the witches’ most of all. The full notes for every speaker come in step 2.

  • Odile, the lamp-seller. A woman of about sixty-five. A light French accent worn soft by years on the road. Low-middle pitch, even and unhurried, a little husky. Dry, precise, fond. To audition with: “Hold it still. There. See how it sits? That’s a good wick.”
  • Tully, the gate keeper. A woman in her fifties. Flat, careful Northern English. Low pitch, slow, sparing. Every word counted out like a coin. Warmth arrives late and small. To audition with: “Name. Coin. In you go. Next.”
  • The Grey Hat. A man of about forty-five. Low and a little weathered, a faint east-coast Scots burr. Slow, clipped, deadpan. Says less than he means. To audition with: “Rope’s wet. Sky’s clear. Nobody touches the wheel but me.”
  • Nettie, the herb-seller. A woman of about sixty. Soft West Country English (all of Wickhollow sounds like this). Middle pitch, quick and warm. A scold that melts. To audition with: “Mint for your belly, sage for your throat, and a talking-to for free.”
  • Io, the Herbal Witch. A woman of about thirty. Warm, low-middle, gentle and unhurried, with a faint trace of Wickhollow under years of travel. Soft, with steel under it. To audition with: “Hold still. This will sting, and then it won’t.”
  • Kore, the Jester. A woman of about thirty. Bright and quick, a trained stage voice that can drop to a hush. Laughter always close under the words. To audition with: “Everyone laughs at the fool. That’s how she gets close enough to tell the truth.”
  • Europa, the Fox. A woman in her late twenties. Light, quick and playful, middle-high, a smile in it. A slight Irish lilt. To audition with: “Snip, there. Oh, don’t sulk. It’s a better shape now.”
  • Callisto, the Ranger. A woman in her mid twenties. Low, calm and measured. Few words, each one meant. A faint accent like nobody else in the game. To audition with: “Wait. There. Now.”

24 lines, 289 words, about 2.0 minutes of speech. The longest line is 18 words; the average is 12. Names said aloud: Bogmire, Io, Kore, Thornwood, Wenna.

What I changed from the brief, and why

  • Line 2: "from behind, at the edge of its light." A note in Moon Faire put the moon straight over the Faire. If it were overhead, the Faire's land would be in the middle of its moonlight, not at the end. Following from behind keeps Chris's two rules (the Faire follows the moon, and the moon is in its sky) and makes every visit a goodbye.
  • Line 3: "seven years ago," one year after her sister. Nettie needs a number to say. Io waited a year for her sister, then left. That is why she has not been back: the house is full of someone who is not there.
  • Line 5: the moon is sinking and the fire is nearer. The brief said what the fire does but not why it is worst now. A low moon is a weak pull. This ties the fire to the land's last years without a new rule of the world.
  • Line 8: Europa sews the flames down here, in zone 1. It comes from Moon Faire's own story. It gives the Grey Hat a reason he can fly while the flames still lean, and it means the hand that sewed the flames down at the start is the hand that cuts them free at the end.
  • Line 9: the oldest letter is given by hand. The brief does not say what becomes of Halcyon. This is a big call, so it is new call 10 below. No zone 1 line needs it.
  • Smaller: Callisto sees first (line 6), Nettie and the garden (line 7), the moth flies toward the moon (line 8).

Each witch's thread

  • Io holds on. Home, the cottage, her sister, the letters. In zone 1 she comes home after seven years, finds her garden kept by someone else and her sister's boots still by the door, and wants every lamp brought back. What she learns: to let go. Not to lose, but to let go: she sends the letters, hands the oldest to her sister, and leaves home again, this time saying goodbye.
  • Kore lets go. She has been below and walked back out laughing, and she sees how every story ends. The fire stops endings, so she cannot see anything it holds. New: tonight, as the flames lean, she looks at Io and for the first time in her life cannot see Io's ending. She has never had anything she could not bear to lose, until Io. In zone 1 she laughs at the flying lamps, watches the moth when Io watches the lamps, and toward the fen begins, without saying why, to stand in front of Io. What she learns: to defend.
  • Europa mends. She sees the call as threads pulling every flame, and she can sew a flame to its wick with blue thread (the ribbon-dyer sold her all his blue). In zone 1 she sews the Grey Hat's twelve flames so the ship can fly. What she learns: some cloth has to be cut.
  • Callisto waits for the moment. She is the first to see the flames lean. She is a princess of a land the moon left long ago (call 4), so she is the only one of the four who knows the dark can be lived in. At Bogmire, already dark, she is the one who notices that people are managing. New, because the brief gave her none: what she learns is to say it aloud. She has always seen first and said little. At the end she is the one who tells Halcyon what she knows: it is a season.

The witches say little in zone 1 (46 lines between them), at set moments: the opening, the cottage, the Thornwood's edge, the first sight of the fen, before and after the great wraith, the leaving. Io and Kore disagree gently at most of them.

The four people the story is told through (zone 1)

Each has a beginning, a turn and an end across the stages. Every ordinary person has one line that sends the coven to one of them, by a place they can find.

  • Odile, the lamp-seller (the camp). She reads flames and measures the lean in fingers' widths. Begins: every flame on her stall leans with no wind, and she sends the coven to the captain. Turns: the lean grows, and she puts her shutters up, because she will not sell a lamp she cannot promise will stay. Ends: after Bogmire the lean is a finger less, and the shutters come down.
  • The Grey Hat (the jetty). Begins: he has "lost his whole crew", the twelve lamps, and he does not lift dark. Turns: as they come home he hangs each one, and once, briefly, says why: he flew dark once, when he was young. Ends: all twelve home and sewn, the flames still leaning. Any sailor with sense would fly the other way. He flies them toward it.
  • Nettie, the herb-seller (Wickhollow). Begins: she knows Io at once, and scolds her: seven summers watering her garden and not one letter. Turns: she tells what she saw of the sisters, the heartsease Halcyon bought from her the morning she went north, "for you". Ends: she lets Io go. She will keep the garden. Gardens wait better than people.
  • Old Wenna (Bogmire). Begins: she sells herbs by touch in a town gone dark, and is not as frightened as she should be. Turns: she tells what took the fen's lamps and where it sits, and that it did not seem cruel, only lost. Ends: the lamps are home and she can see her jars again, and she admits she had got used to the dark. It was honest.

Where the old games' lore fights with this, and my way through

  • Moon Faire: the moon is straight over the Faire (a Claude note, not Chris's word). That would put the Faire in the middle of a land's moonlight. My way: it follows from behind, at the edge (line 2).
  • Moon Faire: the Faire stands somewhere for one night, then moves on; the four are due on stage. Here the Faire has pitched its tents for the land's last nights. Nobody in zone 1 says how long it stays.
  • The new Envoi's proposal: the Faire's ship lifts, Io fights alone, and Io and Kore meet on the first night. Chris has since said all four are together from the start with a shared past. My way: they have known each other for years. Kore's "first time she sees nothing" moves from their first meeting to tonight, looking at Io. (The fights job still has Envoi's first fight as Io's alone, on the bridge. That is not settled, and nothing here builds on it.)
  • The new Envoi: Io keeps Wickhollow's Moonwell, and Mayor Gretch offers it for rest. That is call 5. Zone 1's lines leave every Moonwell in Wickhollow out.
  • The new Envoi: Quill flies the ship, an old skiff with a name. Chris has made the Grey Hat the captain. Quill is a sailor with a stall, and nobody says the ship's name.
  • Moon Faire: wisps are flames gone cold; the new Envoi: souls going hollow. Settled by "a soul is a light". Nobody outside the coven knows which is true, and each guesses from their own trade.
  • Moon Faire: the Jester joins later in the story. Chris's newer word wins: all four from the start.
